# Responsibilities of an Engineering Manager (Fine-Grained)

## 1. People Leadership & Team Health

- Recruit, hire, and retain engineers; build a balanced team (levels, strengths, domains).
- Run onboarding plans (first week/month/quarter), assign buddies, ensure early wins.
- Set clear role expectations for each person (scope, ownership, behaviors, impact).
- Hold regular 1:1s (weekly/biweekly): career goals, feedback, blockers, morale, motivation.
- Coach engineers on execution, communication, technical judgment, and collaboration.
- Provide timely feedback (in-the-moment + structured) and document patterns.
- Facilitate performance reviews: gather evidence, calibrate, write assessments, deliver outcomes.
- Create growth plans: skills to build, projects to pursue, mentoring/support needed.
- Sponsor promotions: set criteria, help build a packet, ensure scope matches level.
- Address underperformance: diagnose root cause, set improvement plan, follow through.
- Manage team dynamics: resolve conflict, mediate disagreements, prevent toxicity.
- Foster psychological safety: encourage questions, admit mistakes, reward learning.
- Maintain engagement and motivation: recognition, purpose, autonomy, mastery.
- Manage workload sustainability: burnout detection, PTO coverage, reasonable expectations.
- Ensure inclusion: equitable opportunities, fair distribution of visible work, bias mitigation.
- Develop senior talent: mentorship, delegation, leadership opportunities, succession planning.
- Plan org design at your scale: pods/squads, ownership boundaries, interfaces.
- Maintain team rituals: standups (if used), retros, planning, demos, learning sessions.
- Handle sensitive issues: complaints, HR processes, confidentiality, ethics.

## 2. Delivery Ownership (Execution Management)

- Translate goals into executable plans: milestones, dependencies, risks, staffing assumptions.
- Ensure work is well-shaped: clear problem statements, success criteria, constraints.
- Drive planning ceremonies (as appropriate): quarterly planning, sprint planning, backlog grooming.
- Ensure estimation discipline (or forecasting discipline) and communicate confidence ranges.
- Track progress (not micromanaging): status signals, burndown/throughput, milestone health.
- Remove blockers: cross-team coordination, priority conflicts, access issues, unclear ownership.
- Manage scope: negotiate MVP, phased delivery, reduce complexity, protect critical path.
- Make tradeoffs: speed vs quality, build vs buy, short-term vs long-term.
- Ensure stakeholders get predictable updates: what shipped, what slipped, what changed, why.
- Manage delivery risks: identify early, create mitigation plans, escalate with options.
- Build "execution muscle": postmortems for planning misses, improve process iteratively.
- Maintain operational cadence: weekly status, monthly business reviews, OKR check-ins.

## 3. Technical Leadership (at the Management Layer)

> Often "hands-off keyboard," but still accountable for technical outcomes.

- Set and uphold engineering standards: code quality, testing, CI, release practices.
- Ensure sound architecture and technical direction in your area (via tech leads/staff eng).
- Review key technical proposals for alignment: scalability, reliability, maintainability, cost.
- Ensure appropriate technical decision-making process: RFCs, ADRs, design reviews.
- Balance feature work with technical health: tech debt payoff, refactors, platform investments.
- Build the right ownership model: service ownership, on-call ownership, runbooks.
- Ensure security and privacy practices: threat modeling, secrets handling, least privilege.
- Manage technical risks: dependencies, vendor risk, legacy constraints.
- Set quality bars: what "done" means, rollout criteria, rollback plans.
- Create/maintain incident readiness: monitoring, alerting, SLOs, error budgets (if used).

## 4. Product Partnership & Outcome Accountability

- Partner with Product/Design/Data to define outcomes, not just outputs.
- Clarify user/customer problems; validate assumptions; ensure engineering context is represented.
- Contribute to roadmap creation: sequencing, feasibility, cost, risk, staffing constraints.
- Negotiate priorities and timelines; explain tradeoffs with concrete options.
- Ensure clear acceptance criteria and measurable success metrics for initiatives.
- Maintain tight feedback loops post-launch: adoption, performance, user pain, support tickets.
- Drive iteration: decide what to improve, what to stop, what to expand.

## 5. Stakeholder Management & Communication

- Identify stakeholders: product, execs, sales, support, security, finance, legal, other eng teams.
- Set expectations early: scope, timeline, risks, decision points, dependencies.
- Communicate status with the right level of detail for each audience.
- Escalate effectively: bring options, recommendation, impact, and "ask."
- Manage conflict between stakeholders (e.g., competing priorities) and broker agreements.
- Represent your team upward: advocate for resources, protect focus, explain constraints.
- Represent business context downward: explain "why," connect work to goals.

## 6. Operational Excellence (Reliability, On-Call, Incidents)

- Establish on-call rotations; ensure fairness, training, and backup coverage.
- Ensure runbooks and playbooks exist and are kept current.
- Ensure monitoring/alerting quality: actionable alerts, low noise, proper ownership.
- Drive incident response: roles, comms, timelines, decision-making, customer updates.
- Run blameless postmortems: root cause, contributing factors, follow-up actions, owners/dates.
- Track operational KPIs: incident frequency, MTTR, change failure rate, availability, latency.
- Maintain release safety: feature flags, canaries, progressive rollouts, rollback readiness.
- Manage reliability investments: capacity planning, load testing, resilience improvements.

## 7. Process Design & Continuous Improvement

- Define lightweight processes that fit the team stage (startup vs scaling vs mature).
- Improve developer experience: tooling, CI speed, local dev setup, documentation.
- Establish coding/review norms: PR size, review SLAs, ownership, quality gates.
- Ensure effective retrospectives: convert learnings into changes with follow-through.
- Measure and improve flow: lead time, cycle time, WIP limits, queues, handoffs.
- Reduce coordination tax: better interfaces, clearer ownership, fewer meetings.
- Build knowledge-sharing systems: docs, tech talks, onboarding guides, internal wikis.

## 8. Planning, Prioritization & Capacity Management

- Own capacity planning: headcount vs roadmap; highlight gaps early.
- Allocate time across:
  - Roadmap delivery
  - Operational work
  - Tech debt
  - Support requests
  - Learning/development
- Maintain a healthy backlog with correct ordering and clear "ready" criteria.
- Manage interrupts: define intake, triage, SLAs, and protect maker time.
- Handle staffing changes: backfills, reassignments, contractor strategy (if relevant).
- Plan for contingencies: attrition, outages, urgent requests, dependency slippage.

## 9. Budgeting & Cost Management *(varies by company)*

- Forecast and manage headcount budget (open roles, start dates, comp bands inputs).
- Manage cloud/vendor costs in your area: spend visibility, cost-saving initiatives.
- Make build/buy/partner recommendations with total cost of ownership (TCO).
- Justify investments: ROI narratives, risk reduction, opportunity cost.

## 10. Culture, Values, and Environment

- Model expected behaviors: accountability, curiosity, respect, transparency.
- Reinforce engineering values: quality, ownership, collaboration, customer focus.
- Set norms around communication: async-first where possible, meeting hygiene.
- Encourage craftsmanship: testing, readability, simplicity, docs.
- Create space for learning: training time, mentorship, experiments, tech exploration.
- Recognize contributions publicly and fairly.

## 11. Talent Systems & Hiring Execution

- Define role needs: leveling, competencies, interview loop design.
- Write/maintain job descriptions aligned to actual work.
- Source candidates (or partner with recruiters): outreach, referrals, networking.
- Train interviewers; ensure consistent evaluation and good candidate experience.
- Debrief hiring loops; make decisions; close candidates; manage offers.
- Maintain a hiring pipeline forecast; reduce time-to-fill.

## 12. Career Development, Leveling & Compensation Inputs

- Ensure leveling clarity: scope, impact, autonomy expectations for each level.
- Calibrate performance across managers/teams (fairness and consistency).
- Provide compensation input: market context, internal equity, performance differentiation.
- Support internal mobility: transfers, role changes, growth into tech lead/manager paths.

## 13. Cross-Team Execution & Dependency Management

- Map dependencies early (upstream/downstream), create integration plans.
- Align on APIs/contracts/interfaces; drive versioning and rollout coordination.
- Negotiate staffing and timelines across teams; ensure shared accountability.
- Manage integration risk: staging environments, contract tests, compatibility plans.

## 14. Metrics, Reporting & Accountability

- Define and track team success metrics:
  - Delivery predictability
  - Quality (defects, escapes)
  - Reliability (SLOs, incidents)
  - Velocity/throughput (used carefully)
  - Customer outcomes (activation, retention, NPS, etc.)
- Produce periodic reporting for leadership (weekly/monthly/quarterly).
- Use metrics to drive improvements—not to punish—while still holding standards.

## 15. Decision-Making & Prioritization Frameworks

- Decide when to be directive vs empower others to decide.
- Establish decision rights: who decides, who consults, who informs.
- Use structured tradeoff tools: impact vs effort, RICE, cost of delay, risk matrices.
- Ensure decisions are documented (ADRs/RFCs) and revisited when assumptions change.

## 16. Managing Technical Projects *(program-style, at team scale)*

- Ensure strong project leadership (tech lead/PM); fill gaps if missing.
- Break initiatives into phases with clear gates (design complete, MVP, GA).
- Ensure testing strategy exists: unit, integration, load, security, UAT.
- Coordinate releases and communications: docs, enablement, migrations, deprecations.
- Ensure post-launch ownership: monitoring, support, iteration backlog.

## 17. Customer Support, Escalation & Feedback Loops

- Establish how the team handles support tickets/bugs: triage, severity, SLAs.
- Participate in major customer escalations: coordination, comms, root cause, fix plan.
- Close the loop with Support/Sales/CS: known issues, workarounds, roadmap clarity.
- Ensure high-quality documentation for internal and external consumers (as needed).

## 18. Governance, Compliance, and Risk *(domain-dependent)*

- Ensure compliance requirements are met: SOC2, HIPAA, GDPR, PCI (as applicable).
- Participate in audits: evidence collection, control ownership, remediation plans.
- Ensure secure SDLC practices: reviews, scanning, dependency management, access controls.
- Manage data handling and privacy reviews for new features.

## 19. Leadership Within the Broader Engineering Org

- Contribute to org-wide standards and strategy discussions.
- Mentor other leads/managers; share best practices.
- Drive initiatives beyond your team: platforms, reliability, hiring, DEI, tooling.
- Represent engineering in cross-functional leadership forums.

## 20. "Manager as Multiplier" Daily Responsibilities *(practical reality)*

- Prioritize your own time: avoid being a bottleneck; delegate deliberately.
- Keep your calendar healthy: protect focus blocks for thinking and writing.
- Write clearly: proposals, updates, decision docs, narratives.
- Maintain "situational awareness": who is stuck, what is risky, what is unclear.
- Spot patterns early: repeated incidents, recurring delays, morale dips, quality regressions.
- Build systems so the team runs well without heroics.
