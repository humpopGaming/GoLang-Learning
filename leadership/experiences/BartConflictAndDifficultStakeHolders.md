### **S — Situation**

My team was consistently struggling to deliver work on time. One key factor was friction with a Principal Engineer, Bart.

He would frequently block pull requests, requesting additional work outside the original scope. While some of his suggestions improved code quality, others introduced unnecessary work, and his communication was often slow.

This created:

- Delivery delays
- Frustration within the team
- Reduced predictability in sprint commitments

---

### **T — Task**

My responsibility was to:

- Remove this bottleneck
- Protect team delivery and morale
- Maintain appropriate engineering standards
- Resolve the situation without damaging the working relationship

---

### **A — Actions**

### 1️⃣ Understand both sides

- Spoke with the team to understand their experience and concerns
- Reviewed the code and PR feedback myself to form an objective view
- Spoke directly with Bart to understand his intent

---

### 2️⃣ Acknowledge valid concerns (this is key for senior stakeholders)

- Recognised that some of his feedback was valuable and aligned with maintaining a clean codebase
- Challenged areas where I felt the requests were **out of scope or disproportionate**

---

### 3️⃣ Create a clear working agreement

I introduced a structured approach to balance quality and delivery:

- Defined what types of changes were:
    - ✅ Required within the story
    - ⚠️ Optional or future improvements
- Agreed a **time-boxed approach**:
    - The team would address feedback within a reasonable limit
    - If additional work was still required beyond that:
        - Bart would need to **invest his own time** to coach and guide the team

---

### 4️⃣ Shift from blocking to coaching

- This reframed the interaction:
    - From **gatekeeping** → to **mentorship and enablement**
- Encouraged more collaborative discussions rather than one-sided PR blocking

---

### 5️⃣ Maintain alignment

- Set up **regular check-ins** to:
    - Reinforce the agreement
    - Adjust where necessary
    - Ensure the relationship remained productive

---

### **Conflict Element**

- This required challenging a **more senior engineer**
- There were differing views on:
    - What “good quality” meant
    - How much work was appropriate within a story

I handled this by:

- Using **evidence (code + impact on delivery)**
- Acknowledging his expertise
- Framing the discussion around **team outcomes, not personal opinion**

---

### **R — Result**

- PR cycle time improved as unnecessary blocking reduced
- The team regained **confidence and momentum**
- Delivery became more predictable
- Bart became more engaged in **coaching rather than blocking**

This created a better balance between:

- Maintaining quality
- Enabling consistent delivery

---

### **L — Learning**

- Strong engineers can unintentionally become **bottlenecks if expectations aren’t aligned**
- Clear agreements are essential to balance **quality and flow**
- As an EM, part of the role is to **mediate between different priorities and create effective working boundaries**
- Influencing without authority requires **respect, evidence, and collaboration**
