### **S — Situation**

I was leading a team called Protostar that was struggling with delivery.

- Output was **consistently low and unpredictable**
- Morale was declining
- Learning and progression had stalled
- The team was in a **negative cycle**, where unexpected issues and interruptions made each sprint harder than the last

This lack of predictability made it difficult to plan effectively and created **risk to feature delivery**.

---

### **T — Task**

My goal was to:

- Stabilise the team
- Improve delivery predictability
- Increase team confidence and morale
- Create a sustainable way of working

---

### **A — Actions**

### 1️⃣ Diagnose the real problem (not just symptoms)

Instead of imposing solutions, I worked with the team to understand the root causes:

- Ran sessions to discuss how they felt and what they were experiencing
- Used the **5 Whys** technique to uncover underlying issues

We identified two key problems:

- Estimating work with **insufficient knowledge**
- External interruptions and dependencies **compounding delivery issues**

---

### 2️⃣ Introduced a clear vision: “Boring Sprints”

I introduced the concept of a *“boring sprint”*:

> A sprint where everything is predictable — we understand the work, and there are no surprises.
> 

Then I **challenged the team to define how we achieve that**, creating ownership rather than top-down change.

---

### 3️⃣ Implemented structural changes to improve flow

Together, we introduced several key changes:

### 🔹 Improve readiness of work

- Increased and formalised **spike work**
- Introduced a Definition of Ready focused on **removing unknowns**
- Required **test cases upfront**, improving clarity and quality

### 🔹 Reduce cognitive load (very Team Topologies aligned)

- Reduced the amount of work entering the sprint
- Ensured engineers only attended **relevant meetings**
- Scoped work more tightly

### 🔹 Improve estimation and predictability

- Rebaselined estimation:
    - 1 sprint ≈ 13 points
    - Stories sized relative to developer capacity
- Introduced the **“weight triangle”**:
    - Effort (light)
    - Complexity (medium)
    - Unknowns (heavy → must be removed)

### 🔹 Improve flow and focus

- Introduced **WIP limits**
- Ensured all stories were **fully understood and broken down during spikes**

### 🔹 Fix cross-team blockers

- Facilitated cross-team conversations
- Included key stakeholders (e.g. design/architecture) early to remove blockers

---

### 4️⃣ Reinforce behaviour and culture

- Coached the team on **time management and focus**
- Supported them in adopting new ways of working
- Encouraged ownership — including lightweight, engaging ways to assess work (e.g. internal “difficulty” signals)

---

### **R — Result**

Over **3–4 sprints**:

- Delivery became **significantly more predictable**
- The number of unexpected issues reduced dramatically
- Team morale improved as they regained control and confidence
- Planning became more reliable, enabling **better alignment with stakeholders**

While some external dependencies still required adjustment, the team moved from a reactive state to a **stable, consistently delivering team**.

---

### **L — Learning**

This reinforced that:

- Delivery problems are often **system problems, not people problems**
- Reducing **unknowns and cognitive load** is key to improving flow
- Teams are more effective when they **co-create solutions** rather than being directed
- As an EM, my role is to **design the environment for predictable delivery**, not just manage tasks
