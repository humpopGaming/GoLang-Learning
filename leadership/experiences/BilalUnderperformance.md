### **S — Situation**

I was managing a cohort within our software academy, responsible for preparing engineers to be ready for production teams. One individual, Bilal, was underperforming compared to expectations for his stage.

His **output was low**, and more importantly, his **communication was poor**, which made it difficult for the team to collaborate effectively and created a **risk to overall delivery and team progress**.

---

### **T — Task**

My responsibility was to:

- Bring his performance up to the expected level
- Ensure he could contribute effectively to team delivery
- Do this in a **fair, structured, and supportive way**

---

### **A — Actions**

First, I wanted to understand his perspective.

- I asked him how he felt he was progressing — he believed he was doing well
- I **clearly outlined the gap** between his current performance and expectations, using specific examples

From there, I put in place a **structured improvement approach**:

**1. Clear, measurable goals**

- I set short-term, very specific goals such as:
    - Diagramming end-to-end workflows
    - Explaining how specific patterns were used in the codebase

**2. Increased support and guidance**

- Daily check-ins to:
    - Refocus his attention
    - Help him avoid overcomplicating tasks
- Weekly progress reviews to track improvement
- Pair programming sessions to model expected behaviours

**3. Adjusting the environment for success**

- I gave him **more focused, scoped work** to reduce cognitive load
- Encouraged him to attend a **communication skills course**

**4. Addressing behavioural signals**

- While he verbally indicated improvement, I challenged this constructively when his actions didn’t reflect it
- I maintained **honest, consistent feedback**, even when it was difficult

---

### **Conflict Element (woven into Actions)**

There was a **misalignment in perception**:

- He believed he was progressing well
- I had to **challenge that view with evidence**, while keeping him engaged and motivated

This required **direct but supportive conversations**, ensuring expectations were clear without demotivating him.

---

### **R — Result**

- He made **some improvement**, particularly in understanding and structure
- However, he did not reach the level required to move into a software engineering role

As a result:

- He was **not selected for a development position**, but was able to transition into another department where his skills were better suited
- The team’s delivery was **protected**, as I ensured work was appropriately scoped and supported during the process

---

### **L — Learning (this is what makes it senior)**

This experience reinforced that:

- Underperformance needs to be addressed **early, clearly, and with structure**
- It’s important to balance **support with accountability**
- Not all outcomes are about retention — sometimes success is helping someone find a role where they can perform better
- From a delivery perspective, **protecting team output while supporting individuals is critical**
